Why available fault current matters

Available fault current is part of evaluating equipment interrupting ratings, short-circuit current ratings, labeling, and the suitability of installed electrical equipment. The value can change from the service to downstream locations because conductor impedance affects the calculation.

  • Support single-phase and three-phase calculation workflows.
  • Add multiple downstream panel locations.
  • Use conductor material, size, length, and configuration inputs.
  • Include an optional estimated motor contribution when applicable.
  • Print a formula and calculation summary for review.
Use verified project data. Available fault current work can affect equipment-safety decisions. Confirm utility or transformer information, conductor data, equipment ratings, applicable standards, and project-specific requirements with qualified professionals and the authority having jurisdiction.

Available fault current questions

What information is needed?

The required inputs depend on the calculation point and system, but typically include source information and conductor details such as material, size, length, and configuration.

Can I calculate more than one downstream panel?

Yes. The calculator supports multiple downstream locations so the point-to-point results can be reviewed together.

Does it replace an engineering study?

No. It is a calculation and documentation aid. Project-specific engineering, equipment evaluation, and authority requirements remain the user's responsibility.
